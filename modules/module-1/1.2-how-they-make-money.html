How These Businesses Actually Make Money

The unit economics that make this worth doing — in real numbers, not theory.

Before you pick a niche in Module 2, you need to understand why a local business will pay you monthly for software at all. It's not because they love technology. It's because a working system either makes them money, saves them time they'd otherwise pay someone for, or stops them losing leads they're currently letting slip.

A concrete example

Take a local service business — say, a small HVAC company. Every missed call is a missed job worth $200–$800. If they're missing even 2 calls a week because no one's answering the phone during a job, that's $400–$1,600 a month walking away. A simple booking + follow-up system that costs them $150–$300/month isn't a nice-to-have — it pays for itself many times over in the first missed call it catches.

This is the pitch you're actually making: not "cool AI software," but "this stops money you're already losing."

Copy this

Value-estimate prompt paste into any AI chat, fill in the brackets 
I'm evaluating [NICHE/BUSINESS TYPE] as a potential client type for custom software.

Help me estimate:
1. What's the average value of one customer/job for this business type?
2. What's a realistic number of leads or bookings they might be losing per month to poor follow-up, no online booking, or manual tracking?
3. Based on that, what's a defensible monthly price for software that fixes this?

Be conservative and realistic, not hype-y — I want a number I can actually defend in a sales conversation.

Why this matters for pricing later

In Module 4 you'll pitch a price. That price only holds up if it's anchored to money the business is already losing, not to "what software normally costs." Get comfortable doing this math now, before you're on a call.

What you should have when this lesson is done A completed value estimate (from the prompt above) for one business type you're considering for Module 2.
