How to Actually Pick a Niche

Real selection criteria — not a generic list of industries to pick from.

Before you start this lesson
  • Your location, and any industry background worth trading on
  • 20 minutes with an AI chat open

A niche is good for you if it scores well on three things, not because it sounds exciting or someone else made money in it.

The three criteria

  • Reachable — you can find and contact these businesses directly (they have a phone number, an owner you can identify, a physical location near you or an easy way to reach them).
  • Underserved — most businesses of this type are still running on paper, spreadsheets, or nothing at all. If they already have slick software, you're competing on price against something built for them specifically, which is a much harder sell.
  • Valuable per client — using the math from 1.2, is a missed lead or a manual process actually costing them enough to justify $150–$500/month?

A niche that's exciting but fails all three (say, high-end art galleries — glamorous, but hard to reach, usually already curated, and low volume) is worse than a boring one that passes all three (say, local moving companies — plenty of them, usually running on phone calls and a whiteboard, real money lost to missed bookings).

Copy this

Niche brainstorm prompt paste into any AI chat, fill in your details 
I'm choosing a niche of local businesses to build custom software for. Here's my situation:
- My location/area: [CITY/REGION]
- My background or industry experience (if any): [BACKGROUND]
- What I already know or have connections in: [ANYTHING RELEVANT]

Suggest 8 candidate niches, and for each one, score it 1-5 on: how reachable they are, how underserved they typically are (still on paper/spreadsheets), and how much a missed lead is probably worth to them. Rank them by total score.

The fourth criterion: can you actually build it?

The three criteria above are about the market. This one is about you, and skipping it's how people pick a niche that looks great on paper and then stall for three weeks trying to ship the first build.

Base44 is exceptionally good at a specific shape of software: forms, records, dashboards, scheduling, client portals, internal trackers, anything that's fundamentally structured data with a clean interface on top. That covers the real needs of most local businesses, which is exactly why this opportunity exists.

It's a harder fight when the core need is something else — live video, hardware or POS integration, heavy regulatory compliance, or a mobile app that must ship to an app store. Those aren't impossible, but they're the wrong place to spend your first month while you're still learning the tool.

So add a fourth score to your shortlist: can I ship a credible first version of what this niche needs in a single week? If the answer is no, the other three scores don't matter yet. Park it and come back in month four, when you're fast.

What you should have when this lesson is done A ranked shortlist of 3 candidate niches from the prompt above, ready to test in 2.2 and 2.3.
