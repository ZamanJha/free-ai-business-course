Locking In Your Niche

Turning everything from 2.1–2.3 into one locked decision you commit to for the rest of the course.

Before you start this lesson
  • An active Base44 Pro plan — every build in this course runs on it
  • Your validation results from 2.3
  • The niche you are committing to

Indecision here is the quiet killer of momentum. This lesson exists to force a decision, in writing, that you carry into every module from here forward.

Copy this

Decision-lock prompt paste into any AI chat, fill in your findings 
Here's what I know:
- Candidate niches and scores from 2.1: [PASTE]
- Lead Finder results and validation notes from 2.2/2.3: [PASTE]

Based only on this real data (not on excitement or gut feeling), tell me which niche I should lock in, and write a one-sentence definition of it including the specific area/region I'm targeting. Then give me three reasons I might be wrong, so I go in with eyes open.

Write the resulting one-sentence definition down somewhere visible. Modules 5, 5, and 6 all assume you have a specific answer here, not "local businesses in general."

Set up the niche workspace you will use all year

Committing to a niche is not just a decision, it is a filing system. Do this now, while you have one project, and client number six is a fork instead of an archaeology expedition.

  • Name projects consistently. Something like NICHE — Client Name and NICHE — Template. Sounds trivial until you have fourteen projects and cannot remember which one was the good version.
  • Keep one canonical template per niche. Every time you solve something well for a client, fold it back into the template. That template is the thing that makes your work compound rather than repeat.
  • Keep a scratch project. One place to try things without touching client work. You will use it constantly, and it keeps experiments out of anything a client can see.

None of this is exciting, and it is the difference between a year that builds an asset and a year that produces fourteen unrelated one-offs.

The template is the business Your first client pays for a build. Every client after that pays for the same build plus their specifics, at the same price and a fraction of the hours. Maintaining one good niche template is the single highest-leverage habit of the year.
What you should have when this lesson is done One written sentence defining your locked niche and target area — referenced directly in Modules 5 and 7.
