Contracts & Getting Paid

The scope doc and payment terms that keep a small build from turning into unpaid, unbounded work.

Scope creep is the most common way a first client project goes from profitable to a headache. A short written scope, agreed before you build, prevents almost all of it.

The scope-of-work template

Scope of work template copy, fill in brackets, send before you start building 
Project: [CLIENT BUSINESS] Custom App

What's included:
- [Feature 1, plain English]
- [Feature 2, plain English]
- [Feature 3, plain English]
- Hosting and a live link
- One round of revisions after the first version is shown

What's not included (available as an add-on):
- [Anything you're deliberately excluding]

Timeline: [X] business days from deposit received
Price: $[AMOUNT], due as [50% deposit / 50% on delivery] or [monthly retainer starting at $X/mo]

By approving this, we agree to the scope above. Anything beyond this list will be scoped and priced separately.

Getting paid

Take a deposit before you start building, even a small one (25–50%). It's not really about the money at that size — it's a real signal the client is committed, which filters out the tire-kickers before you've spent your Module 6 sprint week on someone who was never going to pay.

What you should have when this lesson is done Your own filled-out scope-of-work doc, ready to send to client #1 before you start building.
