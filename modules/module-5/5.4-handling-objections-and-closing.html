Handling Objections & Closing

Price-anchoring against the agency alternative, and the responses to the objections you'll actually hear.

Before you start this lesson
  • An active Base44 Pro plan — every build in this course runs on it
  • Your demo from 5.3, and the notes on what they asked for
  • Your pricing from 1.2

The same handful of objections come up over and over. Having a real answer ready — not improvised in the moment — is the difference between losing the call and closing it.

"That's more than I expected to pay"

Anchor against the real alternative: "A dev agency would usually quote $3,000–$5,000 upfront for something like this, plus weeks of back-and-forth. I can have this live in days for a fraction of that, and you've already seen it working, not a mockup."

"I need to think about it"

"Totally fair. What specifically would you want to think through — is it the price, the timing, or something about how it works? " — this turns a vague stall into a real objection you can actually address.

"We already have a system"

"Got it — is it actually solving [the specific problem they described earlier], or is it more that you're used to it? " — reference their own words from 5.3, don't argue against a system you haven't seen.

The three objections that are really about the tool

Most objections in this niche are not about price. They are about risk, and specifically about the fact that you are one person rather than an agency. Three come up constantly, and all three have honest answers.

  • "What happens if you disappear?" Fair question, and the honest answer is strong: it is a real deployed application, their data is theirs, and it can be exported or transferred. They are not buying a dependency on you personally. Say this before they ask it and it stops being an objection at all.
  • "Is this real software, or a template?" It is a real application built for their workflow — which is exactly why it fits better than the off-the-shelf product they already rejected. The fact that you built it quickly is a reason it costs less than an agency, not a reason it is worth less.
  • "Who maintains it?" This is not an objection, it is them asking for the recurring arrangement. Do not talk them out of it. Price it in 5.5 and answer plainly: you are on this platform, building in it every week, and changes are a conversation rather than a change request.

Notice that the credible answer to all three is that you are genuinely set up to be around. That is a real thing about you, not a line — and it is worth being able to say truthfully when someone is deciding whether to trust you with their business.

Continuity is the actual product Small businesses have been burned by the freelancer who vanished. Being visibly committed to the tool for the year is not a sales tactic, it is the thing that makes the recurring work possible — and it answers the only objection that really matters.
