Days 4–7: Turning the Build Into a Sale

Closing with a finished product in hand, using the exact scripts from Module 4.

Before you start this lesson
  • An active Base44 Pro plan — every build in this course runs on it
  • The build from 6.2, deployed and working on their real details
  • Your outreach list and scripts from Module 4

Everything here is Module 4, applied — the difference is you're now demoing their actual finished app, not a rebranded template.

Day-by-day

  1. Day 4: send the demo (reuse the 4.3 call structure) — walk them through their own real, working app.
  2. Day 5: handle objections using your 4.4 responses; if it's a yes, send the final invoice/payment request.
  3. Days 6–7: collect payment, run the 3.4 handoff checklist (custom domain if applicable, access, walkthrough), and formally deliver.

If it's a no

Not every sprint ends in a close. If this one doesn't, the built app isn't wasted — it becomes a second demo template you can show the next prospect, and you go back to your 4.1 list for the next lead. The build time was never wasted; it compounds.

Copy this

Close-day prep prompt paste into any AI chat before the closing call 
I'm about to close [CLIENT NAME] on a [ONE-TIME PRICE / MONTHLY PRICE] deal after showing them their finished app. Remind me of my top 3 objection responses from Module 4.4 adapted to this specific business, and give me one confident closing line to use once they've said yes verbally, to move straight to payment without losing momentum.

Demo the build, do not describe it

Everything in the first three days was in service of this: you are walking into the conversation with a working application, on their business, with their name on it. That changes the nature of the meeting entirely.

Do not open with the pitch. Open the app.

  • Start with their data on screen. Their services, their booking types, their branding. The first ten seconds should be them recognising their own business, not you explaining what they are looking at.
  • Let them drive if they will take it. Someone clicking through their own system is selling themselves far more effectively than you can.
  • Change something live. When they say it should also do X, do X while they watch. This is the moment the sale usually turns, because it stops being a product demo and becomes a preview of working with you.
  • Then talk about price — after they have handled it, not before. The number reads completely differently once the thing is real to them.

If the answer is no, you have lost three days and kept a build that is now a portfolio piece and a template for the next prospect in the same niche. That asymmetry is why this sprint is worth running even when you are not confident about the particular client.

A no still leaves you with an asset The build does not evaporate when the deal does. It goes into your niche template, becomes the screenshot in your next outreach email, and makes the following sprint shorter. Very few sales processes leave you better equipped after a loss.
What you should have when this lesson is done Either a signed, paid client #1 — or a completed close attempt and a second demo asset ready for the next lead.
